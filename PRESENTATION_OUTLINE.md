## SLIDE 2: Strategic Positioning and Business Unit Overview
**Heading:** Ford's Three-Business-Unit Operating Model: Differentiated Strategic Positioning

**Key Talking Points:**
- **Ford Blue (ICE)**: Cost leadership positioning targeting traditional truck market
  - 2024 Performance: 2,279K units, $40.2B revenue, 12.8% EBIT margin
  - Competitive advantage: F-150 brand equity, manufacturing scale, dealer network
  - Strategic challenge: Legacy cost structure, excess capacity

- **Ford Model e (EV)**: Market penetration positioning in growing EV segment
  - 2024 Performance: 3,861K units (largest segment), -131.8% EBIT margin
  - Strategic bet: Scale-up investment today, profitability tomorrow
  - Critical challenge: EV demand slower than projected, competitive intensity high

- **Ford Pro (Commercial)**: Premium services differentiation through integrated software
  - 2024 Performance: 1,503K units, $20.2B revenue, 13.5% EBIT margin
  - Competitive advantage: Telematics integration, fleet management software, professional customer focus
  - Strategic strength: Software-as-a-service expanding

**Visual Elements:**
- **Embed Visualization #1**: Business Unit Performance Dashboard (2024) showing units, revenue, EBIT, margins
- Three columns comparing each business unit positioning, volumes, and margins
- Porter positioning triangle (cost leadership vs. differentiation) with business units placed strategically

**Speaker Notes:**
"Ford deliberately rejected a unified strategy in favor of three distinct business models. This reflects understanding that sustainable advantage requires clear positioning choices and trade-offs. Blue focuses on cost leadership in traditional markets. Model e accepts near-term losses to establish EV market position. Pro combines cost efficiency with premium software services. Each business unit operates with separate manufacturing facilities, profit accountability, and operational strategies."

## SLIDE 3: Operations Strategy Framework Analysis
**Heading:** MIT Decision Framework: Structural and Infrastructure Decisions Enabling Strategic Execution

**Two-Column Layout:**

**Column 1 - Structural Decisions: Facilities, Capacity, Vertical Integration**

- **Facilities & Geography** (375+ operations, 41 manufacturing plants)
  - North America dominance (14 U.S., 3 Mexico, 2 Canada plants)
  - Specialization by business unit (Blue ICE, Model e EV, Pro commercial)
  - Global footprint enabling market access and tariff optimization

- **Capacity & Utilization Challenge**
  - Designed capacity: 5.5-6.0M units
  - Actual production: 3.5-4.0M units
  - Excess ICE capacity from demand shift to EVs
  - Model e capacity underutilized (EV demand slower than projected)

- **Vertical Integration in Batteries**
  - BlueOval SK joint venture (50/50 with SK On)
  - Tennessee, Kentucky plants coming online
  - Multi-year raw material offtake agreements (lithium, cobalt, nickel)
  - Strategic choice: control supply, cost structure, technology vs. capital intensity risk

**Column 2 - Infrastructure Decisions: Workforce, Supply Chain, IT Systems, Organization**

- **Workforce Transformation**
  - UAW 2023 agreement: 25% wage increase (2023-2028)
  - Skills transition: ICE → EV + software
  - Technology talent acquisition from competitors (Tesla, Apple, Google)
  - Training programs and culture integration challenges

- **Supply Chain Strategy**
  - Fulfillment supply chain: 45-day payment terms, dealer-based distribution
  - Supply chain finance program: enables supplier discounting, improves Ford's working capital
  - Dual-sourcing for critical components, geographic diversification
  - Technology supply chain: battery and semiconductor partnerships

- **IT Infrastructure & Digital Capabilities**
  - Manufacturing execution systems (MES), IoT, predictive maintenance
  - Ford+ platform: connected vehicles, OTA updates, telematics
  - Ford Pro software services: fleet management, subscription revenue
  - Cybersecurity investment (NHTSA consent order)

**Visual Elements:**
- **Embed Visualization #2**: Manufacturing Footprint showing geographic distribution (pie chart) and business unit specialization (bar chart)
- **Embed Visualization #3**: Capacity Utilization Trends (2015-2024) showing ICE decline, EV rise, production volume dual-axis
- Decision matrix table (structural decisions vs. business unit strategic fit)

**Speaker Notes:**
"The MIT framework emphasizes that competitive advantage requires coherent integration across structural and infrastructure decisions. Ford's structural decisions—dedicated plants by business unit, battery vertical integration, optimized manufacturing—align reasonably well with strategic intent. However, infrastructure decisions face challenges. Workforce transformation to EV/software skills remains incomplete. Supply chain adapted post-2021 disruptions but faces commodity volatility. IT systems are advancing but Ford Pro's software differentiation requires sustained investment. Strategic fit varies across business units—Blue strong alignment, Model e emerging misalignment, Pro strongest alignment."

## SLIDE 4: Implementation Effectiveness Using SIR Framework
**Heading:** Strategy Implementation Roadmap: Evaluating Execution Excellence Across Seven Pillars

**Seven Pillars Assessment (Traffic Light/Scorecard Format):**

| Pillar | Assessment | Key Evidence | Challenge |
|--------|-----------|--------------|-----------|
| **1. Leadership** | STRONG | Clear Ford+ vision, strategic clarity | Navigate unexpected challenges (labor costs, EV demand) |
| **2. Value Creation** | MIXED | Blue/Pro profitable, Model e destroying value | Model e losses unsustainable beyond 2025 |
| **3. Business Model** | TRANSFORMING | Pro advancing (software/services), Blue traditional, Model e uncertain | Ambidextrous execution across three models |
| **4. Culture** | TRANSITIONING | Legacy manufacturing vs. technology culture tension | Integrating ICE/EV teams and external talent |
| **5. Stakeholder Mgmt** | MODERATE | UAW agreement, dealer relationships, supplier partnerships | Dealer tension (online sales vs. franchise), supplier cost pressure |
| **6. Employee Engagement** | MIXED | Blue uncertain, Model e/Pro growth opportunities | Talent poaching risk from competitors, legacy anxiety |
| **7. Performance Measurement** | STRONG | Robust KPI framework, business unit accountability | Short-term optimization can conflict with transformation |

**Strategic Implications:**

- **Strengths**: Leadership clarity, value creation in core segments (Blue, Pro), measurement discipline
- **Risks**: Model e value destruction unsustainable, culture integration challenging, talent retention threat
- **Critical Success Factors**: Model e achieving cost competitiveness by 2026, Ford Pro software differentiation deepening, Blue capacity right-sizing

**Visual Elements:**
- **Traffic light scorecard** showing green/yellow/red assessment for each SIR pillar
- **Embed Visualization #4**: Operations Decision Categories showing strategic fit alignment scores across Blue, Model e, Pro

**Speaker Notes:**

Blue's excess capacity requires difficult decisions about facility consolidation. Ford Pro represents success story—integrated business model creating defensible margins. ## SLIDE 5: Strategic Recommendations and Conclusion
**Heading:** Strategic Recommendations: Navigating 2025-2026 Transformation Inflection Point

**Four Key Recommendations:**

**1. Accelerate Model e Cost Reduction** (URGENT)
   - Target: Achieve positive EBIT margins by 2026
   - Actions: Battery cost reduction (BlueOval SK optimization), platform efficiency, manufacturing yield improvement
   - Success metric: Battery cost <$120/kWh, vehicle cost competitiveness with Tesla/Chinese competitors
   - Risk: If unsuccessful, reconsider Model e commitment

**2. Deepen Ford Pro Software Differentiation** (GROWTH)
   - Target: Software-as-a-service revenue 15-20% of Pro revenue by 2027
   - Actions: Fleet management features expansion, predictive maintenance integration, customer business system integration
   - Competitive advantage: Create defensible margin expansion and customer stickiness
   - Investment: Continue technology talent acquisition and platform development

**3. Address Blue Structural Overcapacity** (URGENT)
   - Challenge: 12.8% margins mask excess capacity from demand shift to EVs
   - Actions: Strategic plant closures, consolidation, or joint ventures for underutilized facilities
   - Rationale: Excess capacity creates fixed-cost burden undermining profitability
   - Timeline: Decisions needed 2025, execution 2026-2027

**4. Integrated Supply Chain Risk Management** (ONGOING)
   - Lessons: 2021-2022 disruptions revealed supply chain vulnerabilities
   - Actions: Stress-test supply chain across tariff, geopolitical, commodity scenarios
   - Priority focus: Diversify supplier concentration (batteries, semiconductors, raw materials)
   - Integration: Connect fulfillment and technology supply chains

**Overall Conclusion:**

Ford demonstrates clear strategic intent through the Ford+ framework and thoughtful operational decisions. However, implementation effectiveness remains unproven across all business units. Blue operates successfully but faces capacity challenges. Model e represents strategic bet on EV scale-up that market currently contradicts. Ford Pro exemplifies best implementation—integrated hardware/software creating defensible differentiation.

Success requires disciplined execution on three fronts: (1) achieving Model e cost competitiveness and profitability, (2) deepening Ford Pro software differentiation as primary growth engine, (3) right-sizing Blue capacity to match realistic demand. The 2025-2026 period will determine transformation success.

**Visual Elements:**
- **Recommendation dashboard** highlighting priority, timeline, success metrics for each recommendation
- **Strategic fit matrix** (referenced visualization #6 in memorandum) showing strategic alignment vs. implementation effectiveness by business unit
- **Timeline graphic** showing critical decision points (Model e profitability 2026, Blue capacity rightsizing 2025-2027, Pro software scaling 2025-2027)

**Speaker Notes:**
"Ford's transformation strategy is sound—three distinct business units with clear positioning. However, operational execution determines success. Model e is the critical risk. Negative margins of -131.8% are unsustainable. Ford must achieve cost competitiveness and positive profitability by 2026 or reconsider commitment. Blue requires capacity decisions to match demand reality. Pro represents the success story and should receive continued investment. The next 18 months will be defining for Ford's transformation. Strategic clarity is present, but operational challenges are substantial."
