## Version 1: Focused MIT Framework

### Document Structure
- **Executive Summary** (expanded, 1 page) - Includes business unit context
- **Section II: MIT Structural Decisions** (~3 pages)
  - Facilities and Geographic Distribution (condensed)
  - Capacity and Utilization Strategy (core content)
  - Vertical Integration Strategy (battery focus)
- **Section III: MIT Infrastructure Decisions** (~2.75 pages)
  - Workforce and Skills Transformation
  - Supply Chain Configuration
- **Section IV: Strategic Fit Analysis** (1.5 pages)
  - Blue, Model e, Pro alignment assessment
- **Section V: Implementation Highlights** (1 page)
  - Value Creation and Performance Measurement SIR pillars
- **Conclusion** (0.5 pages)
- **Works Cited** (1.5 pages)

### Strengths
✅ **Complete MIT Framework:** Covers all structural and infrastructure decisions per assignment requirements  
✅ **All Business Units:** Maintains comparative analysis showing differentiation  
✅ **Explicit Framework:** Clear academic structure demonstrating framework mastery  
✅ **Balanced:** Appropriate depth across all decision categories  
✅ **Professional:** Maintains memorandum format with proper styling  

### Weaknesses
❌ **Condensed:** Each section feels compressed—less narrative depth  
❌ **Less Strategic Context:** Minimal discussion of competitive dynamics  
❌ **Reduced Examples:** Many operational specifics eliminated to save space  
❌ **Framework-Centric:** May feel formulaic rather than strategic narrative  

### Best For
- Instructors emphasizing explicit framework application
- Students wanting to demonstrate framework mastery clearly
- Assignments prioritizing operations strategy decision categories over strategic storytelling
- Users comfortable with more academic, structured presentation

### Estimated Page Count: **8.75 pages** (can adjust details to hit exactly 8-9)

## Version 3: Integrated Narrative

### Document Structure
- **Executive Summary** (1 page) - Three business units, integrated framework
- **Section II: Ford's Transformation Challenge** (~2 pages)
  - Ambidextrous transformation rationale
  - Strategic differentiation reasoning
  - Market realities confronting intent
- **Section III: Operations Decisions Supporting Business Unit Strategies** (~4.5 pages)
  - **Ford Blue subsection** (1.25 pages) - Cost leadership, capacity challenge
  - **Ford Model e subsection** (1.75 pages) - Market penetration, misalignment
  - **Ford Pro subsection** (1.5 pages) - Premium services, strongest alignment
- **Section IV: Strategic Fit and Implementation Effectiveness** (~2 pages)
  - Alignment assessment by business unit
  - Implementation challenges using SIR insights
- **Conclusion** (0.75 pages)
- **Works Cited** (1.5 pages)

### Strengths
✅ **Narrative Flow:** Most readable version with strategic storytelling emphasis  
✅ **Integrated Framework:** MIT concepts woven throughout rather than separated  
✅ **All Business Units:** Maintains portfolio perspective and comparisons  
✅ **Business-Centric:** Organized around strategy rather than abstract frameworks  
✅ **Accessibility:** Stronger for non-operations audiences; clearer business impact  
✅ **Balanced:** Covers all business units with appropriate depth each  

### Weaknesses
❌ **Less Explicit Framework:** MIT framework present but not explicitly labeled  
❌ **May Seem Less Academic:** Narrative style could be perceived as less rigorous  
❌ **Framework Recognition:** Instructors emphasizing framework labels might not see them clearly  
❌ **Slightly Longer Sections:** Some sections harder to condense in narrative style  

### Best For
- Instructors valuing strategic thinking and business impact over framework mechanics
- Students wanting strongest narrative flow and readability
- Assignments emphasizing business unit strategy and competitive positioning
- Professional audiences (executive summaries, investor presentations)
- Users wanting to demonstrate integrated strategic thinking

### Estimated Page Count: **8.5 pages**

## Decision Framework

### Choose **Version 1 (Focused MIT)** if:
- Your assignment explicitly emphasizes MIT Decision Category Framework application
- Instructor expects explicit framework structure with labeled decision categories
- You want to demonstrate clear mastery of structural vs. infrastructure distinctions
- You prefer academic, structured presentation over narrative flow
- You value completeness of framework coverage over strategic storytelling

### Choose **Version 2 (Model e Deep)** if:
- Assignment allows single-case or business-unit deep dives
- You want to demonstrate exceptional analytical depth on complex challenge
- You believe EV transition is most strategically interesting aspect of Ford
- You're comfortable with narrower scope in exchange for greater depth
- Your instructor values depth-of-analysis over breadth-of-coverage

### Choose **Version 3 (Integrated)** if:
- You want strongest narrative flow and readability
- Your instructor values strategic thinking and business impact
- You prefer business-unit organization over abstract framework categories
- You want to demonstrate integrated thinking (frameworks applied naturally, not imposed)
- You're comfortable with implicit rather than explicit framework labeling
- You want best version for professional or business-oriented audiences
